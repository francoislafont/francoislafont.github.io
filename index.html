I am a PhD candidate (expected 2027) in Economics at CESAER (INRAE) under the supervision of Julie Le Gallo and Marie Breuillé. I'm also a Research Fellow at SMART (INRAE) working on the Horizon Europe project Ruralitic under the supervision of Carl Gaigné and Morgan Ubeda.

In 2025 I visited Albert Solé-Ollé at the IEB/UB in Barcelona. In 2026 I visited Gabriel Ahlfeldt at Humboldt University.

My research interests are in urban, political and environmental economics. I study the interaction of local politics and land-use policies and their consequences on environmental and economic outcomes.

In a previous life, I worked as a Data Scientist for the French Court of Auditors.

Research

Working papers

  • Are Bigger Jurisdictions Better? The Effects of Local Consolidation
    with David Agrawal, Marie Breuillé and Clémence Tricaud · June 2026
    Abstract

    What is the optimal size of a jurisdiction? We study the consequences of increasing jurisdiction size using the 2015 NOTRe reform in France, which forced mergers of small intermunicipal entities. We implement a difference-in-differences design and leverage newly assembled, comprehensive data on public finances, services, employment, and housing transactions. We find no evidence of scale economies. Instead, consolidation triggers a spatial reallocation of public services away from peripheral places and toward political centers, and lowers house prices throughout the territory—by 5.9% after seven years—with the largest declines in the periphery. These results point to welfare losses from larger jurisdictions and to a widening of within-territory inequalities.

  • Land-Use Litigation and the Microgeography of NIMBYism
    with Eulalie Saïsset · November 2026
    Abstract

    As cities grow, building in already populated areas becomes central to housing affordability and land preservation, but also triggers not-in-my-backyard (NIMBY) opposition. To measure it, we construct a dataset from judicial archives covering all construction and land-use litigation in France since 2005. Using an LLM, we extract with high accuracy rich contextual information from over 200,000 court decisions. We document that NIMBY opposition primarily takes the form of individuals challenging housing permits, disproportionately targeting multifamily housing. Consistent with a simple model in which neighbors litigate to protect their property values, appeals to approved housing concentrate in dense and expensive areas. Second-home owners also account for a significant share of litigation, especially in high-end tourist locations. In low-density areas, by contrast, litigation more often comes from owners of undeveloped land appealing permit denials to preserve their building rights.

  • Your Backyard is our Backyard! The economic effects of land-use planning consolidation
    Abstract

    This paper provides new causal evidence on the relationship between government fragmentation, housing supply, and urban sprawl. I compile new data on French intermunicipal mergers to study a reform that required many municipalities to transfer urban-planning authority to a broader community level. The findings show that, when given the choice, municipalities strongly prefer to retain control over land-use decisions. However, when consolidation is imposed, it results in effective cooperation on the development of new planning rules. Land-use regulation becomes more homogenized as the number of active planning documents over a territory decreases by 30%. Building authorizations for denser forms of constructions increase by about 20% in denser and previously constrained areas. These effects emerge gradually, following the timeline of new planning documents, and they are stronger in areas with more constraints on new construction prior to the reform. This provides empirical support for the idea that coordinating land-use at a larger scale helps internalize positive spillovers.
